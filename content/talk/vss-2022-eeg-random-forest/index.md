abstract: We investigated the random forest algorithm as a framework for extracting the most relevant EEG features distinguishing mild Traumatic Brain Injury (mTBI) patients from Healthy Controls (HC) during a Visuospatial Working Memory task. EEG data from 27 mTBI patients and 27 matched Healthy Controls, epoched across three memory phases and decomposed into 5 frequency bands per scalp site, was used to train random forest classifiers of trial accuracy and diagnosis. Models reached up to 98% classification accuracy, with central-parietal beta and posterior-occipital gamma/beta emerging as the strongest diagnostic markers.
